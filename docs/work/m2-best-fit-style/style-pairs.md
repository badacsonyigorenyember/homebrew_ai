# BA to BJCP style pairs

Step 2 scores BA 2026 and BJCP 2021 styles together. When the best fit is a BA style that has a
pair below, step 2 returns the paired BJCP style instead (R6). Every row with a BJCP code goes
into `db/021_style_pairs.sql` (Task 3); rows with `—` stay BA styles.

**Who decided.** On 2026-10-13 the user delegated the decision to Claude: "You should decide
based on mainly look, taste, smell, etc. If they are similar, they should be connected, except if
the metrics are way too off." The user confirmed the four catch-all pairs of the first proposal
(29A, 34C, 30A, 32A for American-Style Fruit Beer, Experimental Beer, Herb and Spice Beer and
Smoke Beer); every other row is Claude's decision, final, with no row-by-row review.

**The rule.** Claude read each style's full guideline text in `ref.beer_style` (overall
impression, aroma, appearance, flavour, mouthfeel, ingredients, history, comments, style
comparison, commercial examples, vital statistics) and compared the beers, not the names.

- **Pair when the sensory profile is similar:** colour and look, aroma, flavour, mouthfeel;
  ingredients and process back this up. A narrower or broader neighbour or a regional variant
  with the same character is a pair, and so is a specific BA style whose defining character is
  what a BJCP catch-all is about (honey beer and 31B, chocolate beer and 30A).
- **Vitals only veto when they are way off:** the ABV ranges or the IBU ranges do not overlap at
  all. Somewhat stronger or more bitter but overlapping is still a pair. An open BA range
  (`30+`) overlaps everything above its lower bound; a missing range neither vetoes nor helps.
  Ranges that miss by one unit at the edge count as touching: BA's lambic, gueuze and
  spontaneous-fermentation styles list IBU 11–23 against BJCP's 0–10, while both describe
  bitterness as very low.
- **One BJCP style per BA style:** where two fit, the closer one by look and taste.
- **Unpaired on purpose:** `session-india-pale-ale` (brief b21) and `mexican-style-amber-lager`
  (brief b22). Their briefs expect the BA pick, and a pair would turn it into a BJCP one.
- Other `—` rows are catch-alls with no fixed look or taste (session beer, aged beer, other
  Belgian ale, …), beers with no BJCP counterpart, or pairs vetoed by a vitals gap (the reason
  gives the gap).

**Data notes.** A few BA rows carry a cut-off name from the loader (`Export`, `Lager`,
`Light Lager`, `Pilsener`, `Weizen`, `Saison`, `India Pale Ale` twice, `or Oud Red Ale`, …); the
reason gives the full BA name from the guideline text, and the codes are correct. BJCP's Black,
Red, White and other IPA types live inside 21B Specialty IPA, but the stored 21B row holds only
the Brut IPA text and vitals (IBU 20–30).

**Counts:** 151 BA styles paired, 18 unpaired, 169 in all. Sorted by BA code.
Ranges are low–high; `30+` is an open upper end; an empty BJCP range means the style has no
vital statistics (most BJCP specialty styles).
